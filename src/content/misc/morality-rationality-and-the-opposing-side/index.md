## From rationality to correct morality

This builds directly on the earlier note on rationality (see [Rationality & Backtesting Discipline](/mrc-tasks/misc/rationality-and-backtesting-discipline/)). There the mentor places rationality beneath all trading psychology. Here Mr Casino adds a second pillar: **a correct morality**, described as something rationality itself leads to.

**Moral relativism**, as the mentor defines it: "That there is no absolute truth. That morality is subjective and that everyone can define their own version of right and wrong." The mentor compares it to Orwell's "doublethink" (*1984*).

The mentor's position:

- Moral relativism is "absolute bogus". It "requires a lapse of rationality to accept", and is called "probably the largest cause of suffering in the world today".
- **The rationality test:** ask plain questions. Is starvation in the world right or wrong? The senseless killing of people in war? Murder? A mind using its rationality accepts that these are wrong. Refusing that is a failure of rationality, not a difference of opinion.
- **Empathy is strength.** A mind that rejects relativism draws on "the best energy of the human": empathy, a firm stance for justice, a desire for peace. The relativist mind "loses this innate link to the heart", loses itself first, and then causes destruction.
- **Humility:** "No one can ever claim to be perfect. I have my flaws, as do we all." But "we only operate from facts."

## Why it matters to the trader

- Trading "means to follow the psychology of all minds involved."
- Our way of trading is a direct opposing force to a powerful side that holds many advantages. Our minds must therefore be robust, and our edge is a skill that side lacks: **a correct morality**.
- Rationality combined with correct morality lets the mind reach its full potential and become "an unstoppable, unprecedented force", and "united even stronger".
- To reach "the highest levels [of] RR extraction it is a must to be on this path": the rational mind, the correct moral code, and a heart that understands the striving for good.
- **Loyalty is to truth.** "The lessons relate to your trading. Be on truth or do not - your quality of life pays as a result."

**Team standard:** everyone in the team should have a good morality, something to be proud of and to work on constantly. It is only the start of real, powerful change, and that change is primarily for yourself. There are some subjects everyone can agree on; the mentor calls this "the societal advantage we speak of earlier".

## The market is other minds: understand the opposing side

> What are the markets at core? The global price reflection of the many minds of traders engaged who think price is to go up or down.

- **Every trade has an opposing end:** you win, they lose. "To level the playing field you must understand the opposing end of your trade."
- "If you do not understand or accept the minds you are dealing with you can not compete."

In the mentor's model, the minds that matter most are the banks. They control the flow of money and gold, and they are the side whose footprint we follow. See [Fundamentals: USD, the Banks & Gold](/mrc-tasks/misc/fundamentals-usd-banks-and-gold/). The mentor also describes a wider split between a power-seeking financial elite, those who oppose it with rational thought, and those without a firm rational morality who "can be bought/manipulated". The source does not turn this into a specific trading rule.

## Personal practice

- **Perspective:** start by appreciating the blessings others are unable to have. Focus on the true purpose of doing good and eliminate everything else, "for your own power". "Why squabble over trivial affairs."
- **Daily practice:** "Spend time daily envisioning yourself in their place" (those who are suffering). The mentor calls it "a meditation of growth" and your minimum responsibility.
- **Protect your mind:** respect and sympathise with those affected by real suffering, but "do not make it common to flick by such images of reality or inflict your mind with lasting psychological damage." Action matters more. And keep perspective: your own feelings and circumstances are not worse than theirs.
