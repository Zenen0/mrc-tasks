## The model, step by step

The model below is Mr Casino's view of how the monetary system works. It is presented as his framework, not as established fact.

1. **The banking system is the leading power.** The fiat USD is "backed by nothing fundamentally" apart from never-ending debt to draw on, and ordinary citizens feel the economic repercussions.
2. **Control is incomplete.** Controlling the world reserve currency, the base of all transactions and especially of gold buying and selling, did not give a clean sweep. Russia, China and the Arab nations hold their own resources and physical gold and want to end the one-sided monopoly.
3. **Gold price control keeps the system afloat.** "The real fundamentals of the markets, stemming from XAU/USD." Rising spending weakens confidence in the USD through inflation. Competitors, China above all, can offer their own fundamentals-based gold price, and a higher price attracts investors and more physical gold accumulation. So the price has to be constantly adjusted ("manipulated") to allow only "completely accounted for changes".
4. **It all comes down to liquidity.** Fundamentals ultimately act on the physical supply of and demand for gold. If the banks don't move their fixing of XAU/USD to match physical demand in due time, other major countries and players win.

## What the banks do

- "Banks are always short-selling only to buy again at some later date."
- They would like to take price down forever but can't because of competition. Their role is to **manage the required price level** of gold and profit greatly while doing it.
- Their profit comes from controlling all bid and ask prices, with complete access to the exact order flow (the liquidity on both sides), enhanced with AI to target at the most macro level.

## Their limit, and why it matters to us

> There is an extent they are comfortable with before fundamental factors, namely for buys, overpower.

The banks can hold gold down only so far before fundamental buying pressure overrides them.

- The general day trader can't use this knowledge because they can't view the market "from the true lens of RR extraction".
- For us, it is **"the last step in securing the bank's complete footprint"**, and it supports **full swing confident holds**.
- The aim is to see the banks' "printing press of all the markets stemming from USD and then stemming from XAU control exactly as they do."

## On the chart: FU and the fractal approach

- "Every move of price action shown is via all markets' base programmed nature based on liquidity manipulation via FU." FU is the manipulated high/low (definitions and charts: [Terminology](/mrc-tasks/reference/terminology/#fu)); see [Task 1](/mrc-tasks/tasks/1/assignment/) and Task 2's objectives.
- FU is "our weapon to study the true market order flow from the naked eye."
- From it comes the **fractal approach** to extraction: scalp, intraday or swing. The link to XAU manipulation is always involved, so "we always follow the true value trace of price".

## Reading news against the chart

This is the key practical point from the timeline below. On the Nasrallah news:

> Although buys are already reflected in liquidity + overall setting, this incident made buys more important to prepare for market open and mitigation [of] new incidents over the weekend.

- **The bias comes from the liquidity read first.** The fundamental event reinforced a buy bias that was already in the chart. It did not create it.
- **Fundamentals raise conviction in holding.** Here the news made it more important to be positioned for buys into the market open and to cover new incidents over the weekend gap.
- The source gives **no fundamentals-based entry or exit rule** beyond this. Fundamentals support holds and bias; the chart (liquidity, FU) stays the basis.

Week 3 puts it more strongly: "All news is an excuse for volatility" ([Liquidity · News is an excuse for volatility](/mrc-tasks/reference/liquidity/#news-is-an-excuse-for-volatility)).

## Worked example: 2023-24 events and gold

Mr Casino's recurring pattern: war and escalation mean more spending and inflation pressure on the USD, so gold must go higher. Each event below is paired with his stated reading of its effect. The readings are his interpretation, not verified cause and effect.

- **Oct 7th attacks, then Israel's assault on Gaza:** "We see gold price never returns to this date (that gap signifies their loss)." In his reading, a gap that price never returns to marks a loss for the banks.
- **War funding on top of the Ukraine/Russia conflict:** "Every bullet spent has its cost." Gold has to go higher to manage the inflation effect of all the spending on the USD.
- **Hezbollah joins from Lebanon:** further escalation.
- **Houthis (Yemen) disrupt shipping lanes:** everything becomes more expensive, with less supply of physical goods and more spending against them.
- **Global protests, with China and Russia highlighting what he calls the world's hypocrisy:** "The USD power is further questioned globally."
- **Iran directly attacks Israel for the first time:** major escalation risk. Attempts to pacify the situation worked that time.
- **Iran's president Raisi dies in a helicopter crash:** further tension.
- **Assassination attempt on Donald Trump:** tensions higher still. He promises more on this later.
- **CrowdStrike global IT outage (about a week later):** "The markets were certainly affected." He asks whether it was a test for a future event.
- **Hamas leader Haniyeh assassinated in Iran:** retaliation expected, which means heavy spending and an "instant rise in gold price".
- **An American/Turkish citizen killed in the West Bank:** Turkey threatens to get involved.
- **Pager and radio explosions in Lebanon:** "Global security is undermined. Gold prices go higher."
- **Nasrallah killed; open war with Lebanon:** "Gold price shooting up before market closure - the fundamental at play." See *Reading news against the chart* above.

## The wider picture: challenges to USD dominance (late 2024)

*From his later "Miscellaneous" post (late Oct - early Nov 2024, inferred).* This extends step 2 of the model above ("control is incomplete"). Each point separates the observable context from his reading and his forecasts, which were made as of late 2024.

**The frame.** "Although the banking power is vast and strong, much of the world has caught on." There are many resisting powers: "Some are whole countries, some financially, some militarily, some via proxies, some via freedom movements - there are many, but not united - each with their own benefits in mind only, some causes more just than others." They are not a single bloc, and not all of them are on the right side in his view.

**Russia**
- *Context:* the war in Ukraine continues, and Russia remains under Western sanctions.
- *His reading:* "Russia is no ordinary 'enemy' but a self-sustaining superpower of its own. Each day their strength grows is a threat to the world's USD banking dominance."

**China, Taiwan and BRICS**
- *Context:* the US and China are in an ongoing trade and technology conflict. BRICS exists and has discussed alternatives to settling trade in USD.
- *His reading:* "Already in economic war, a Chinese-Taiwan conflict would be most disastrous for the world economy (supply of major goods disrupted/major war costs)." This is the supply-shock channel: fewer goods and higher war spending, the same mechanism as the Houthi shipping disruption above.
- *Forecast:* "China knows of this and so likely prepares for that moment to strike when it is most advantageous. They will let the USD hegemony fall naturally until then - BRICS being one means."

**North Korea**
- *His reading:* "a wildcard that possesses immense destruction potential, with a formidable military power", with "some powerful allies (China/Russia - for now)".
- *Forecast:* "A build-up for an invasion of South Korea is on the horizon."

**Wider instability**
- *Context:* the 2024 Bangladesh uprising, the India/Pakistan/Kashmir conflict, and Afghanistan under the Taliban since 2021. He lists these as part of a broadly unstable world.

**The Middle East as the centre**
- "It all leads back to the present age", above all the war in Gaza, "a war that spills over the whole Middle East (a significant portion of the world and thereby everyone else)."
- *Forecast:* it "will only escalate for peace is not the motive", relations are "beyond repair", and "an inevitable major change is undoubtedly coming."

**What this means for the model.** Nothing here is a trading rule. It widens the backdrop behind the worked example: every rival that grows stronger, and every war or supply shock that raises spending, puts more pressure on the USD and so, in his framework, more upward pressure on gold that the banks have to manage. As with the news section above, the bias still comes from the chart first. The macro backdrop supports conviction in holding.

His broader views on the banking system, power and the Middle East from the same post are in [Mr Casino's Worldview: Banking, Power & History](/mrc-tasks/misc/mr-casinos-worldview-banking-power-and-history/).

## 2025: tariffs, debt and the dollar (Fundamentals series)

*From his Fundamentals series: Part 1 (July 2025) and Part 2 (autumn 2025, inferred).* As in *The wider picture*, each point separates the background from his reading and his forecasts. The geopolitical side of each point is on the [series page](/mrc-tasks/misc/fundamentals-series-geopolitical-state-of-the-world/).

**Tariffs (2 April 2025)**
- *Context:* Trump's "Liberation Day" tariffs started a global tariff war.
- *His reading:* "From a trading "insider" perspective it has been interesting to see the economic battle to keep the USD afloat (and ultimately gold suppressed). It is nothing but buying them time."

**The "big beautiful bill"**
- *Context:* the One Big Beautiful Bill Act was signed on 4 July 2025. The Congressional Budget Office estimated it would add about $3.4 trillion to deficits over 2025-2034.
- *His reading:* "a short term USD gain, longer term adding trillions to national debt." In the terms of step 1 of the model, more debt behind a USD "backed by nothing fundamentally" apart from debt.
- He also suspects "Trump to be poorly advised, by those who want the fall of the US ("Edom")". He does not explain "Edom".

**China's real size and its gold**
- *His reading:* "Their economy is in actuality the largest, only not officially yet due to the USD dominance - yet have heavily invested into raw materials and resources, like gold."
- *Context:* China is the largest economy measured by purchasing-power parity (IMF) and the second largest in nominal US-dollar terms, so the answer depends on the measure; "due to the USD dominance" is his reading. The People's Bank of China was a leading buyer of gold in 2022-24. This extends step 2 of the model ("control is incomplete").

**Rate cuts, then "the dissolving of the Federal government"**

> The dissolving of the Federal government (naturally its timing came after rate cuts- and many were positioned for it) likley aimed at buying them time to pacify the markets and the demise of USD dominance.

- *Context:* the Fed made its first 2025 rate cut on 17 September. "Dissolving" most likely refers to the US government shutdown that began on 1 October 2025 (inference; he does not name it).
- *His reading:* the market was already positioned for the cuts, and the government event buys time "to pacify the markets" and delay "the demise of USD dominance".

**Venezuela**
- *Forecast (autumn 2025):* "A potential invasion of venezuela (a country rich in oil and a certain ever sought after shiny metal … - gold)."
- *Context:* the US began a large naval and military build-up in the Caribbean in August 2025. His reading is the resource motive: oil and gold.

**Iran: "the economic route"**
- *Forecast:* a new attack is "unlikely before other events as they have chosen the economic route - this new war will trigger many other nations and probably the catalyst for the final stage this time of all out full blown war". The "economic route" is his phrase; he does not spell it out.

**Qatar**
- *Context:* Israel struck Hamas officials in Doha on 9 September 2025. *His reading:* "geopolitical alliances were shaken."

### Forecasts revisited

His late-2024 forecasts in *The wider picture* above, set against the 2025 posts:

- **China:** "wait for the right time, to minimise their own risk" repeats his late-2024 forecast that China "prepares for that moment to strike when it is most advantageous".
- **India/Pakistan:** listed in late 2024 as part of a broadly unstable world. In May 2025 it became a four-day conflict between the two nuclear-armed states (7-10 May, after the 22 April Pahalgam attack).
- **The Middle East:** he forecast it "will only escalate for peace is not the motive". In 2025 Israel struck Iran (June) and Hamas officials in Doha (September).

### What this means for the model

His 2025 points are the same pressures as steps 2 and 3 of the model: rising debt and spending behind the USD, rival powers accumulating gold, and war risk. Each one weakens confidence in the USD and so, in his framework, adds upward pressure on gold that the banks have to manage and "buy time" against. **He gives no trading rule here, and no gold call.** The rule from *Reading news against the chart* stands: the bias comes from the chart first, and the macro backdrop supports conviction in holding.

*Market context (background):* gold set successive record highs through 2025, passing $3,500 an ounce in April and $4,000 in early October, and the US dollar index fell nearly 11% in the first half of the year, its weakest first half since 1973. This fits his model's "tolerance limit" (the banks can hold gold down only so far); it is not evidence of suppression.

<summary>Background (editorial, not Mr Casino's words): the mechanisms he mentions</summary>

- **Tariffs.** A tariff is a tax on imports, usually paid by the importer and often passed on in higher prices. Economic theory expects tariffs to strengthen the importing country's currency, but in practice they can also weaken it if they raise fears about growth, trade retaliation or policy uncertainty.
- **National debt.** When a government spends more than it collects in taxes, it runs a deficit and borrows the difference by selling government bonds (for the US, Treasuries). Each year's deficit adds to the total national debt, and the interest on that debt becomes a further cost.
- **Rate cuts.** A central bank cut lowers the interest paid on cash and short-term bonds in that currency, which makes holding the currency less attractive to investors. Gold pays no interest, so lower rates also reduce what an investor gives up by holding gold instead.
- **Government shutdowns.** A US shutdown happens when Congress does not pass funding in time: non-essential federal work stops and many staff are sent home or work unpaid until funding is restored. Official economic data releases can be delayed while it lasts.
- **Sanctions.** Sanctions restrict a country's trade, finance or access to assets, usually to pressure its government without military action. They can cut export income, limit access to the dollar-based payment system and weaken the target's currency.
